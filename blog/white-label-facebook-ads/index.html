White label Facebook ads: how it works for agencies

By Rihards Zeila · 12 November 2026

White label Facebook ads means another team runs your clients' Meta campaigns while your agency keeps the client, the brand and the margin. Here is how it works in practice, what to check before you sign, and where it doesn't fit.

What white label Facebook ads means

A white label (or "white-label") partner does work that your agency sells under its own name. In paid social, that means a specialist team plans, builds, runs and optimises Facebook and Instagram ads for your clients, and your agency presents the results as its own.

Your client never deals with the partner. They brief you, they pay you, and they receive a report with your logo on it. The partner charges your agency a fixed fee, and the difference between that fee and what you bill is your margin.

Agencies usually look for this when one of three things happens:

  • A client asks for ads and you don't run them. Branding, web, design, video and PR agencies hear this all the time. Saying no sends the client to another agency, which then pitches for the rest of the work.
  • Your paid social person is full or away. One specialist can only run so many accounts well, and holidays and sick days still happen.
  • You priced a hire and it didn't add up. A full-time media buyer needs enough accounts to stay busy from month one.

How the handover works

The work moves in a loop, and your agency sits at the front of it.

In practice, the first two weeks look like this:

  1. Brief and access. You share the client's goals, budget and assets. The client (or you) gives the partner access to the Meta Business portfolio as a partner, so nothing changes hands.
  2. Tracking check. Before any money is spent, the pixel, Conversions API and the events that count as a result are checked. Bad tracking is the most common reason paid social "doesn't work".
  3. Build and launch. Campaigns, audiences and creative tests go live. You approve the plan before launch if you want to.
  4. Optimise and report. The team adjusts budgets and creative each week, and you get a branded monthly report to forward or present.

Who owns the ad account

The client should. Meta's business tools let an outside team work in an ad account through partner access, without owning it. That matters for three reasons:

  • If you ever change partner, the account, its history and its pixel data stay with the client.
  • The client's payment method stays on their own account, so your agency isn't financing ad spend.
  • Access can be removed in one click, which makes the arrangement easy to trust.

Be wary of any partner that wants to run your clients' ads from its own ad account. It makes leaving expensive, and it hides the numbers from you.

How to choose a partner

  • Ad accounts stay with the client, through partner access.
  • An NDA and a GDPR data processing agreement are offered before you share client data.
  • Real proof: Ads Manager screenshots, not just logos and percentages.
  • Reporting you can rebrand, ready to forward without editing.
  • One named contact and a stated reply time.
  • No lock-in: monthly terms, and you can take an account back in-house whenever you like.
  • Honest scope: a partner that says "this budget is too small to test properly" is worth more than one that says yes to everything.

Questions agencies ask

Is white label Facebook ads management legal?

Yes. Subcontracting is normal agency practice. Put the partner under an NDA and a GDPR data processing agreement, and make sure your own client contract allows subcontractors.

Will my client find out?

Not from the partner. The client deals only with your agency and receives reports under your brand. In Meta's tools the partner's business name can be visible to people who check account access, so if that matters, tell the client you work with a specialist team.

What is the difference between white label and outsourcing?

Outsourcing is the broader term for handing work to an outside team. White label is outsourcing where the work is delivered under your agency's brand and the outside team stays invisible to the client.

Does a white label partner also do creative?

Many do. White Label Media can write ad copy and direct creative, and also works with the creative your own team makes, which suits design and video agencies.
