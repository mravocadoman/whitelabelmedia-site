Hire a media buyer or outsource paid social? The real cost

By Rihards Zeila · 12 November 2026

The salary is only the start. What decides whether an in-house paid social hire pays off is how many client accounts one person can run, every month, from the day they start. Here are the numbers for four markets.

What a paid social hire costs

Public salary data for "media buyer" and "paid social manager" is thin and inconsistent. Sources disagree by up to two times, and some UK pages rest on a handful of profiles from years ago. So we use the same published figures as our calculator, for comparable social media and performance marketing roles:

"Employer costs" is an assumption you should replace with your own: in the UK, for example, employer National Insurance is 15% above the threshold and the minimum auto-enrolment pension contribution is 3%, before equipment, software and training.

Hire vs partner at five accounts

Take an agency with five clients running Meta ads. One specialist could handle all five. A white-label partner at €450 per account costs €2,250 a month.

The gap is widest where salaries are highest. It narrows in the Netherlands, where the published median is lower, but the partner still costs less at this volume.

The break-even point

The useful question isn't "is a hire expensive?" but "how many accounts must one person run before a hire costs less per account?" Divide the monthly cost of the hire by the number of accounts they run and compare it with the partner's fee.

Twelve or more active accounts per person is a heavy load if each one gets weekly optimisation, new creative tests and a monthly report. We plan around eight accounts per person, which is why our calculator uses eight by default. Change it to match your own team.

Costs the salary doesn't show

  • Recruiting. Agency fees or weeks of the founders' time, and a hiring process that can run for months.
  • Ramp-up. A new hire is paid in full before they are productive.
  • Empty capacity. If you lose a client next quarter, the salary stays the same.
  • Cover. One specialist means no paid social during holidays or sick leave, unless you pay for a second person or a freelancer.
  • Tools. Reporting, creative and analytics software per seat.

None of these are in the charts above, so the real gap is usually larger than shown.

When hiring is the right call

A white-label partner isn't always the answer. Hire when:

  • you have more than about a dozen steady paid social accounts per person you'd hire,
  • paid social is the core of what your agency sells, not an add-on,
  • you need someone in your office every day for client workshops and pitches.

Questions agencies ask

How much does a media buyer cost in the UK?

Published averages vary widely. For comparable UK social media roles Digital Waffle reports an average of £46,267 a year (2026). Add employer National Insurance, pension and tools on top of the salary.

How many ad accounts can one media buyer manage?

It depends on budgets and how much creative testing each account needs. We plan around eight active accounts per person. Past a dozen, weekly optimisation and reporting get hard to keep up.

Is outsourcing paid social cheaper than hiring?

Below roughly 12 accounts per person in the UK, a partner at €450 per account costs less than one hire, before counting recruiting, cover and empty capacity.
