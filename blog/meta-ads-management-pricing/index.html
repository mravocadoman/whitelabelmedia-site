How to price Facebook and Meta ads management for your clients

By Rihards Zeila · 12 November 2026

Paid social pricing goes wrong in two ways: too cheap to do properly, or tied to a number the client can't see the reason for. Here are the common models, what each one rewards, and how to set a price that leaves a healthy margin.

The four pricing models

ModelHow it worksGood forWatch out for
Flat monthly feeOne fixed fee per account per monthPredictable work and budgetsScope creep as spend grows
Percentage of ad spendA share of what the client spends on adsLarge, growing budgetsRewards spending more, not results
HybridA minimum fee, or a fee plus a percentage above a spend levelMost agenciesNeeds a clear spend threshold in the contract
Performance-basedPaid per lead, sale or target hitMature accounts with reliable trackingYou carry the risk of the product, offer and website

Which model to use

For most agencies adding paid social, a flat fee with a spend ceiling is the easiest to sell and to deliver: the client knows the cost, and you agree in writing that the fee is reviewed once monthly spend passes a set level. A pure percentage of spend is easy to explain but leaves small accounts unprofitable and invites the question "are you recommending more budget because it's good for us, or for you?"

Performance pricing sounds attractive, but results depend on things you don't control, such as the product, the price, the website and stock. Use it only on accounts with proven tracking and a track record.

What the fee should cover

Write the scope down, so a price is a price and not the start of a negotiation. A typical monthly paid social retainer covers:

  • Campaign structure, audiences and budgets on Facebook and Instagram
  • Pixel, Conversions API and event checks
  • A set number of new ad variations or creative tests per month
  • Weekly optimisation
  • A monthly report and a short call

Things to price separately: new creative production (video shoots, design), landing pages, extra platforms such as TikTok or Google, and set-up for brand-new ad accounts.

Pricing mistakes to avoid

  • Pricing below the work. If the fee can't pay for weekly optimisation and testing, the account will be neglected, and the client will leave.
  • Including ad spend in your invoice. Keep the client's card on the client's ad account. It keeps your cash flow and VAT simple and the numbers transparent.
  • No spend threshold. A €2,000 account and a €40,000 account are different jobs. Say in the contract when the fee changes.
  • Hiding the partner's role from your contract. Your client agreement should allow specialist subcontractors under NDA.

Before you set prices, check the reporting side too: what a monthly white-label report should contain.

Questions agencies ask

Should I charge a percentage of ad spend for Facebook ads?

It works for large, growing budgets, but on small accounts a percentage rarely pays for the work. A flat fee with a spend threshold, or a minimum fee plus a percentage, is safer.

Should ad spend go through my agency's invoice?

Usually not. Let the client pay Meta directly from their own ad account, and invoice only your management fee.

What margin can I make reselling Meta ads management?

With a €450 partner fee, billing €1,000 leaves €550 per account (55%) and billing €1,500 leaves €1,050 (70%). Your own price decides the margin.
